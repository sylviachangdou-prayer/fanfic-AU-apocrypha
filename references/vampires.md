# Vampire AU Guide

Use this guide for vampire fiction informed by Twilight, The Vampire Diaries, The Originals, Interview with the Vampire, or The Vampire Chronicles. Never merge their rules by default.

## 1. Select a vampire lineage

Before plotting, answer:

- Which source continuity or original rule family applies?
- How is a vampire made, and who is responsible for the new vampire?
- What does blood do: sustain, intoxicate, heal, empower, transmit memory, or create bonds?
- What do daylight, fire, injury, age, invitation, faith, herbs, magic, and decapitation actually do?
- Which mental, sensory, or physical powers exist, and how do they vary?
- Can vampires sleep, eat, reproduce, enter homes, compel, or appear in records?
- What evidence can humans detect?

Write a one-page physiology contract and do not add a new weakness or power at the climax.

## 2. Source-family signatures

### Twilight family

Preserve the distinctive combination of transformed mineral-like bodies, venom, permanent physical state, sleeplessness, predatory sensory intensity, exceptional individual gifts, diet visible through the eyes, newborn volatility, and secrecy enforced by the Volturi. Sunlight creates exposure rather than ordinary combustion. Coven size, chosen diet, mate bonds, and creator responsibility organize social life.

Story pleasures: overwhelming embodiment, restraint versus appetite, family as a constructed coven, law enforced by gifted elites, romance under permanent transformation, and the danger of being seen.

### The Vampire Diaries and The Originals family

Preserve magical origins, transition after death and feeding, compulsion, vervain resistance, daylight vulnerability, witch-made daylight jewelry, invitation rules tied to home ownership, blood as healing and dependency, lineage politics, and the interdependence of vampires, witches, werewolves, hunters, and hybrids. The Originals adds dynastic family conflict, sirelines, New Orleans territory, faction governance, and power sustained through bargains with other supernatural communities.

Story pleasures: reversible alliances, loophole magic, family betrayal, faction balance, city territory, resurrection and imprisonment, and intimate knowledge weaponized over centuries.

### Interview with the Vampire and Vampire Chronicles family

Fix book or screen continuity. Preserve the maker-fledgling bond, predation as moral and aesthetic crisis, unequal power by age and blood, memory across historical eras, covens and theatrical performance, secrecy, confession, unreliable narration, and the tension between companionship and possession. Powers and sunlight resistance vary by age and continuity; verify rather than generalize.

Story pleasures: sensual horror, unreliable self-portrait, historical loss, maker-child conflict, beauty alongside cruelty, performance, and the loneliness or tyranny of memory.

### Original or hybrid family

Choose one source family for physiology and one for social architecture. Record every borrowed element. Explain contradictions through history, species, magic, geography, or deliberate propaganda rather than silent convenience.

## 3. Physiology contract

Track:

| Domain | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Transformation | Agent, stages, duration, mortality, responsibility, failure |
| Hunger | Onset, escalation, sensory change, judgment, recovery |
| Feeding | Quantity, frequency, source, consent, contamination, storage |
| Healing | Speed, scars, retained injury, poison, starvation effects |
| Daylight | Exposure mechanism, timing, protection, social consequences |
| Power | Baseline abilities, individual variation, training, countermeasure |
| Death or containment | What works, who knows, evidence left behind |
| Reproduction and bonds | Biological, magical, social, legal, or absent |
| Mind | Compulsion, telepathy, memory, dreams, resistance, false belief |

Make bodily rules visible in behavior: room choice, clothing, refrigeration, travel, scent management, work shifts, medical avoidance, and who may witness feeding.

## 4. Feeding economy

Blood supply creates the most reliable institutional plot.

Map:

- Source: hunting, donors, hospitals, slaughterhouses, prisons, paid networks, family provision, tourism, or synthetic substitutes.
- Acquisition: who recruits, screens, transports, stores, and conceals.
- Quality: disease, drugs, magic, age, fear, compatibility, and status distinctions.
- Distribution: ration, household control, black market, territory, debt, and emergency reserves.
- Waste and evidence: bodies, records, blood bags, refrigeration, surveillance, missing persons, and cleanup.
- Ethics and class: who can afford consent, who carries risk, and whose disappearance is ignored.

A "vegetarian" or ethical diet must create logistics, temptation, social judgment, and failure conditions; it is not merely a personality label.

## 5. Governance and organizations

Build at least four functions, whether held by one coven or several bodies:

1. **Law and secrecy**: define forbidden exposure, evidence standards, punishment, and jurisdiction.
2. **Territory and feeding**: allocate hunting, donors, routes, and dispute resolution.
3. **Creation and education**: authorize or police new vampires, assign creator liability, and teach rules.
4. **Memory and identity**: maintain genealogies, property, forged documents, trusts, archives, and historical cover.

Optional functions include magical services, daylight protection, diplomacy with witches or werewolves, human intermediaries, medical supply, enforcement, sanctuary, and internal appeals.

Give organizations internal factions: expansion versus secrecy, abstinence versus predation, old wealth versus new numbers, family loyalty versus law, and local autonomy versus central control.

## 6. Time, identity, and memory

- Track legal identities, photographs, biometric change, property transfer, education credentials, taxes, and periodic disappearance.
- Let cities, language, technology, borders, and moral vocabulary change around an immortal character.
- Memory should distort, compress, fetishize, or disappear. Perfect recall across centuries removes drama unless it carries a cost.
- Age creates accumulated relationships and habits, not automatic wisdom or moral superiority.
- Old property depends on human institutions, intermediaries, and records. Immortality does not bypass bureaucracy.

## 7. Human and supernatural relations

Humans may be prey, donors, employees, descendants, lovers, investigators, handlers, legal owners, cultists, physicians, or political shields. Give them leverage. A mortal with access to daylight institutions, records, property, or medicine may control what an immortal cannot.

For multiple supernatural groups, map resource exchange and incompatible law. Avoid a single council that effortlessly governs species with different vulnerabilities and reproduction.

## 8. Character translation

Assign vampire roles through source-character behavior:

- A protective character may become a restrictive maker, ethical donor organizer, or secrecy officer depending on how protection and control interact.
- A status-conscious character may manage lineage, diplomacy, identity, or patronage; do not default to aristocratic fashion.
- An authority-skeptical character may hunt rogue enforcers yet rely on the system's evidence and supply.
- A compulsive planner may excel at feeding logistics while failing under hunger, love, or unjust law.

Decide what immortality magnifies and what it erodes. Preserve contradictions.

## 9. Set pieces

Setting-specific large scenes may include daylight exposure during a public event, a donor system collapse, a coven trial, a territory feast, mass transition, siege across human and supernatural jurisdictions, archive fire, lineage rupture, hospital raid, ritual imprisonment, or a city celebration used as feeding infrastructure.

Give the viewpoint a concrete objective smaller than the spectacle. Track crowd flow, exits, time to sunrise, blood availability, witnesses, compulsion limits, and the evidence left afterward.

## 10. Failure audit

Reject or revise when:

- Rules from different franchises have been mixed without declaration.
- Blood exists only as erotic imagery and has no supply chain.
- Every vampire is rich, elegant, pale, sexually dominant, and politically connected.
- Ancient characters speak as generic aristocrats and possess perfect modern competence.
- Human institutions never notice missing people, property anomalies, or identity failures.
- A romance erases creator power, compulsion, age, dependency, or consent consequences.
- A coven or council has authority without enforcement, information, resources, or dissent.
- The climax introduces a new gift, cure, loophole, or weakness.
