# Canon and Fandom Source Grounding

Use this workflow whenever the user names a franchise, requests canon accuracy, or asks for an AU informed by fandom practice.

## 1. Source layers

Keep these layers separate in notes and never collapse them into one authority:

1. **Primary canon**: the exact books, films, episodes, games, or other original works selected by the user.
2. **Supplemental canon**: official companion writing, official websites, scripts, interviews, or reference books the user accepts.
3. **Adaptation canon**: a screen, stage, or game continuity that may contradict the primary canon.
4. **Documented fact reference**: reliable indexes, encyclopedias, wikis, timelines, maps, and episode guides used to locate facts.
5. **Fanon and reception**: recurring interpretations, tags, pairings, conventions, and contested readings in fan communities.
6. **User AU**: explicit divergences and additions. This layer outranks all earlier layers inside the requested story.

Do not call a popular interpretation canon. Do not call an official adaptation detail book canon unless the user chose a hybrid.

## 2. Research sequence

For a named franchise:

1. Fix continuity, cutoff, edition or adaptation, and supplemental sources.
2. Fix the time slice inside the opening event. For every dynamic fact, distinguish not yet happened, happening now, and already completed.
3. List the exact facts the requested premise depends on. Do not research the entire franchise indiscriminately.
4. Check primary or official sources first when accessible.
5. Use a reliable reference to verify dates, titles, relationships, geography, rules, and appearances.
6. Sample fandom sources only for fanon, trope vocabulary, audience expectations, and alternative readings.
7. Record contradictions and select the version the story will use.
8. Mark unverified facts as uncertain or ask the user when the choice would alter the plot.

Research must answer story questions: who can act, who knows what, what an institution can enforce, how a rule fails, how long movement takes, and what a violation costs.

## 3. Source card

Create a compact card for each relevant work or source family:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Canon layer | Primary, supplemental, adaptation, reference, or fanon |
| Continuity and cutoff | Exact version and endpoint |
| Relevant mechanics | Rules that change action |
| Institutions | Authority, dependencies, internal conflict |
| Character evidence | Decisions and behavior, not labels |
| Signature pleasure | What readers expect to feel |
| Contradictions | Conflicting versions or ambiguous facts |
| AU use | Preserve, translate, reverse, or omit |
| Copy boundary | No distinctive wording, scene replication, or substitute text |

## 4. Canon conflict rules

- Apply the user's latest explicit instruction first.
- Treat accepted manuscript events as true even if the outline differs.
- Let an explicit AU divergence override canon and propagate its effects.
- Prefer the chosen primary continuity over an adaptation.
- Use supplemental canon only when the user includes it.
- Treat reference sites as indexes, not creative authority.
- Treat fandom consensus as evidence of reception, not factual proof.
- Never repair a canon contradiction silently. Choose, record, and remain consistent.
- Do not convert ambiguous event timing into a settled current state. Preserve branches until the opening establishes which action has occurred.

## 5. Fandom sampling

Use Archive of Our Own, FanFiction.net, LOFTER, forums, recommendation lists, or other fanwork sources only when accessible and relevant. Do not attempt to ingest a whole platform.

Sample across different authors, publication periods, pairings, ratings, languages, and popularity levels. Top-ranked works alone overrepresent established ships and older platform dynamics. Prefer tags, summaries, author's notes, public discussion, and user-provided excerpts for mapping conventions. Read full works only when access and the user's rights or the site's terms permit it.

Extract:

- Recurring AU premises and relationship contracts.
- Canon divergence points.
- Common fanon and known points of reader disagreement.
- Genre expectations, pacing, rating, and ending promises.
- Failure patterns readers complain about.

Do not extract reusable sentences, distinctive metaphors, or a single author's voice. Do not present platform frequency as literary quality.

## 6. AU translation

Translate functions before surfaces.

| Source function | AU question |
|---|---|
| Character's status | What grants or denies equivalent leverage here? |
| Source ability | Which skill, access, burden, or reputation serves the same dramatic function? |
| Family or faction | Which institution recreates the obligation without copying the costume? |
| Source conflict | Which AU rule makes the same value collision unavoidable? |
| Iconic object | What object carries equivalent use, memory, and social meaning? |
| Relationship history | Which debts, injuries, rescues, and misreadings survive the transfer? |

After translation, test each major character with three choices. If a different character could make the same choices without changing the scene, the AU has preserved names rather than characterization.

### Two AU modes

Settle which mode the request wants before using the table above.

**Setting transfer** keeps the source's ability system and moves it to a new stage. The table applies in full.

**Character-only crossover** moves the people and nothing else. This is the default for 星际 / 现代 / ABO / 娱乐圈 AU, and the row for source ability does not apply: do not find an equivalent for magic, bending, chakra, a Force, or any other native power system. Delete it. In a Draco-and-Harry interstellar story there is no magic and no substitute for magic.

In this mode:

- **Discard**: the power system, its training institutions, its sports and rituals, its proper nouns, and any physiology that only exists to support it.
- **Keep**: personality vectors, relationship history (who owes whom, who saved whom, who has misread whom for years), the *relative* social gap between characters, formative fears, and speech habits.
- **Rebuild**: the institutional pressure that produced the original relationship has to grow out of the new world's own rules. A pure-blood family becomes a shipping dynasty or a military lineage with its own logic, not a pure-blood family wearing a spacesuit.

Two tests. First, if a rule in the new world translates cleanly back to a rule in the source, it is a reskin — delete it and build one that produces consequences the source never had. Second, every capability a character holds must be fully explained by the new world: what they trained in, who paid for it, what it costs them. A character who is mysteriously gifted at the new world's equivalent of their old power has not left the source.

### Proper-noun rent

Every canon proper noun in an AU must pay rent: it is used, complained about, traded, misread, damaged, or acted upon. A name that is only mentioned is decoration.

Count them. Five franchise nouns in one opening page reads as a costume rather than a world, and the reader registers effort instead of place. Downgrade the decorative ones to generic terms. The names that survive become more visible, not less, because a generic term beside them works as a control.

When a canon noun and a plain noun would serve equally well in a slot, take the plain one.

### Original characters inside canon

For an original character placed in an existing continuity, fix their position in the canon geography before any interaction with canon leads: where they sit, who put them there, what they already know from living in that place, and which canon figures are merely scenery to them.

Introduce canon leads first as observed background rather than as scene partners. An original character noticed by a canon lead on their first appearance reads as wish fulfilment. One who watches a canon lead do something ordinary reads as a resident.

Give the original character a faculty the canon cast lacks or holds differently, then describe the canon world through it. A difference in perception integrates an original character far better than a difference in power.

## 7. Copyright and style boundaries

- Use source works for facts, mechanisms, characterization evidence, themes, and transformation.
- Do not reproduce long passages, reconstruct unavailable chapters, or continue from a user-provided excerpt by copying its wording.
- Abstract third-party style samples into measurable features. Never store full copyrighted works inside the skill.
- Match voice at full fidelity when the user supplies or owns the samples, including their humor, harshness, and figurative habits. For a third party's style, work from craft properties the user names rather than from remembered text. In both cases the boundary is verbatim wording, never style itself.
- Keep fanfiction transformative. If the user intends commercial publication, replace protected characters, terminology, and distinctive world elements with original equivalents.

## 8. Final source check

Before drafting, verify:

1. The selected continuity and cutoff are explicit.
2. The exact time slice inside any ongoing canon event is explicit.
3. Canon, adaptation, fanon, and AU inventions are labeled separately.
4. Every plot-critical rule has a source or is marked as an AU rule.
5. Character anchors come from behavior and decisions.
6. The story does not depend on an unverified title, date, relationship, power, institution, or current possession.
7. The AU creates new causal consequences instead of replaying a source scene with altered nouns.
