# Relationship Trope Engine

Treat a trope as an emotional contract and pressure pattern, not a substitute for plot. Let the user choose. If the user does not choose and the request is relationship-centered, select one primary trope from character history and one optional secondary trope from the world's institutions.

## 1. Selection variables

Choose through these variables rather than random preference:

- Starting bond: strangers, rivals, enemies, colleagues, friends, lovers, spouses, family, teacher-student, ruler-subject.
- Existing injury or debt.
- Power asymmetry and whether it is public, private, legal, economic, magical, bodily, or informational.
- Emotional knowledge: who knows, who denies, who misreads, who performs indifference.
- Public status: legitimate, secret, forbidden, mistaken, contractual, or impossible.
- Desired ending: union, qualified repair, separation, corruption, sacrifice, tragedy, or ambiguity.
- Consent and content boundaries.
- Time scale: immediate attraction, repeated proximity, years of history, reincarnation, or displaced timelines.

The chosen trope must worsen or complicate the premise before it rewards the reader.

## 2. Antagonistic and competitive bonds

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Bickering adversaries | Friction becomes attention; competence is recognized before affection | Insults without real disagreement or consequence |
| Fated or professional rivals | Both want mutually exclusive versions of the same future | One rival becoming incompetent to protect the other |
| Love and attempted destruction | Desire and legitimate lethal or political conflict remain simultaneously true | Violence treated as flirting without injury or moral cost |
| Opposing-lane partners | Repeated direct contest produces intimate knowledge and grudging reliance | Endless banter with no changed strategy |
| Arch-enemies to closest allies | A shared threat exposes compatible values while old injuries remain active | Immediate trust after one rescue |
| Closest allies to enemies | A value fracture turns privileged knowledge into a weapon | Betrayal caused by a trivial misunderstanding |
| Grievance-entangled love | Love grows inside inherited, political, familial, or personal hatred | Hatred disappearing once attraction is admitted |

## 3. Friendship and loyalty bonds

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Intimate friendship | Familiarity, shared labor, private language, and chosen loyalty carry the story | Friendship used only as a waiting room for romance |
| Childhood companions | Old access conflicts with adult difference and unequal memory | Nostalgia replacing present-day compatibility |
| Unexpected arrival | A new person disrupts a long-established bond or self-concept | The newcomer winning solely through novelty |
| Long-married familiarity | Routine becomes a field of tiny permissions, irritations, and practiced rescue | Making stability equivalent to boredom |
| Lifelong companionship | Survival across eras depends on repeated recommitment, not one vow | A promised happy ending without historical cost |

## 4. Separation, regret, and return

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Reunion after a broken bond | Repair requires confronting the original structural cause | Chemistry erasing betrayal, abuse, or incompatible goals |
| Regretful pursuit after harming a partner | The pursuer loses entitlement and must accept consequences without guaranteed reward | Suffering used as automatic absolution |
| Reverse regretful pursuit | The former rejecter or deserter must act without controlling the injured person's response | A public spectacle replacing repair work |
| Love that cannot be possessed | The bond is real but timing, duty, death, identity, or values prevent union | Misery sustained by one avoidable conversation |
| Brief encounter | Intensity comes from a known deadline and changed life afterward | Treating brevity as proof of depth without specific intimacy |
| Beautiful but unreal bond | Projection, dream, performance, memory, or impossible conditions create intimacy that cannot survive ordinary reality | Revealing that nothing mattered because it was unreal |
| Good beginning, ruined ending | Early grace generates the exact conditions of later collapse | Sudden tragedy unrelated to accumulated choices |

## 5. Devotion, longing, and concealment

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Silent protection | Material acts accumulate while motives remain deniable | Protection that removes the other person's agency |
| Hidden love | The reader tracks costly concealment and misread evidence | Endless stasis without decisions |
| Open pursuit | One person states desire while respecting a meaningful refusal | Persistence portrayed as entitlement |
| Corrupt person's sincere exception | One genuine attachment collides with an otherwise harmful life | Love being used to declare the person secretly good |
| Virtuous person's selfish exception | A principled person discovers whom or what they will compromise for | One selfish act erasing an established moral structure |
| Unattainable ideal | A person is loved as distance, standard, or symbol before being known as human | Idealization rewarded without correction |
| Mistaken substitute | Resemblance to a lost person creates access, suspicion, and injury | The substitute lacking a separate identity |
| Secret relationship | Concealment creates asymmetric risk, coded routines, and public misrecognition | Secrecy used only for erotic atmosphere |
| Publicly sanctioned union | Ceremony, witnesses, law, family, and reputation make commitment materially consequential | A wedding functioning as the end of all conflict |

## 6. Social and institutional bonds

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Marriage before love | Contractual duties and shared logistics create observation, debt, and choice | Cohabitation automatically producing affection |
| Teacher and student | Knowledge, authority, succession, rebellion, and eventual separation structure the bond | Eroticizing an active minor-adult or coercive dependency |
| Ruler and subject or master and servant | Formal obedience conflicts with private leverage and unequal exposure | Treating servitude as decorative devotion |
| Dominant executive and protected spouse | Economic command meets domestic dependence, performance, and resistance | Wealth solving every conflict or infantilizing the spouse |
| Older and younger partner | Different life stages, authority, mortality, and social perception affect the relationship | Age difference used as personality |
| Matched powers | Mutual competence creates trust, rivalry, and the danger of escalation | Identical skill sets and interchangeable voices |
| Biological siblings as taboo bond | Kinship, prohibition, family system, secrecy, and harm are inseparable from desire | Ignoring coercion, developmental history, or social consequence |
| Social siblings or pseudo-siblings | Chosen or imposed kinship labels conflict with an adult relationship | Using "not biologically related" to erase the family history |

For teacher-student, age-gap, kinship, coercive, or dependent relationships, establish adult ages where intimacy is involved, make consent legible, and preserve the consequences of power. Follow platform policy and the user's boundaries.

## 7. Time, fate, and resemblance

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Reincarnated lives | Recognition competes with the right to become different people | Past-life destiny erasing current consent |
| Displaced timelines | People meet with unequal histories, ages, grief, or knowledge | Time travel used only to repeat canon with foreknowledge |
| Love at first sight | Immediate perception creates a risky commitment that later knowledge must test | Attraction being treated as complete understanding |
| Affection through duration | Repeated ordinary contact changes attention and access | A montage replacing the actual turning points |

## 8. Healing, corruption, and endings

| Trope | Relationship engine | Cheap failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Late renewal | A person thought finished acquires a new appetite, duty, or future | Love curing trauma, disability, grief, or age |
| Redemption through relationship | The bond creates occasions for accountable choices but cannot perform redemption for the person | Forgiveness granted before restitution or changed conduct |
| Mutual ruin | Each person knowingly enables the other's worst adaptation because separation feels worse | Glamour without material, moral, or bodily cost |
| Enduring ordinary life | The climax tests whether they can build and repeatedly maintain a shared life | Domesticity presented as static reward |

## 9. Power and consent

For coercive-love plots, decide what the work is promising: horror, dark romance, tragedy, escape, corruption, negotiation, or eventual freely chosen intimacy. Do not slide between these modes without signaling the change.

Track:

- Who can refuse and what refusal costs.
- Who controls movement, money, medicine, status, secrets, or bodily autonomy.
- Whether compliance, attachment, survival strategy, and consent are being distinguished.
- Which actions cannot be repaired by attraction.
- What the narration endorses, questions, or leaves unresolved.

Intensity is not produced by deleting consequences.

## 10. Arc construction

Build a trope arc through six movements, varying their size and order as needed:

1. **Baseline**: Establish the current access, trust, debt, misreading, and public status.
2. **Proof under pressure**: One person reveals a behavior the other cannot dismiss.
3. **Private exception**: Grant access or protection that contradicts the public relationship.
4. **Fracture**: Force the trope's central contradiction into a consequential choice.
5. **Irreversible act**: Someone pays, exposes, betrays, refuses, returns, or relinquishes control.
6. **New equilibrium**: Union, repair, separation, tragedy, or corruption changes what each can now ask of the other.

Every movement must appear through action, logistics, institutional consequence, or changed behavior. Do not label the relationship stage in narration.

## 11. Combination rules

- Use one primary trope to define the emotional contract.
- Use at most one secondary trope to create complication or temporal shape.
- Let the setting supply pressure: marriage law, rival houses, medical dependence, mission orders, reincarnation records, school authority, or corporate ownership.
- Reject combinations that require a character to abandon immutable anchors without a causal transformation.
- Do not run several trope arcs as a checklist. Merge only when the same choice advances both.
