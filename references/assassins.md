# Assassin and Espionage AU Guide

Use this guide for hitman, assassin, secret-agent, rival-agency, handler, extraction, or impossible-mission AUs. Focus on fictional plausibility and narrative consequence, not real-world instructions for harming people or evading law.

## 1. Choose the source lane

- **Solitary professional**: routine, handler dependence, isolation, domestic vulnerability, and one relationship that compromises clean procedure.
- **Rival killers and double lives**: competing employers, cover identity, professional equality, secret marriage or partnership, and orders that turn intimacy into exposure.
- **Deniable team mission**: specialized crew, state or quasi-state sponsor, briefings, disguises, infiltration, reversal, extraction, and institutional abandonment.
- **Court or historical assassin**: patronage, household access, guild, oath, poison culture, succession, religious legitimacy, and slow communication.
- **Speculative assassin**: magical marks, psychic bonds, identity systems, synthetic bodies, vampire law, or interstellar jurisdiction. Load the relevant setting guide as well.

Select one primary lane. A lone killer, married rival professionals, and a state mission team operate under different social and plot structures.

## 2. Source-work signatures

### Leon: The Professional

Study the contrast between lethal routine and limited domestic life, the intermediary who controls work and money, spatial intimacy in apartments and corridors, the professional's emotional underdevelopment, a dependent relationship that changes his priorities, and a recurring ordinary object that carries the possibility of roots. Do not reproduce the film's scenes or erase the severe age and power problem in its central relationship.

### Mr. and Mrs. Smith

Study two high-competence professionals whose separate employers and cover marriage produce double ignorance, domestic irritation mirrored by tactical conflict, equal threat, mutual recognition through combat, and employers who turn the pair into liabilities. The engine is not merely "married assassins"; it is whether any part of a relationship built through concealment was real.

### Mission: Impossible

Study team specialization, deniable authority, briefings, institutional distrust, identity performance, layered objectives, practical preparation, a plan that must be revised in motion, loyalty chosen after official support disappears, and set pieces organized around time, space, access, and extraction.

Do not combine all signatures into one generic "secret organization."

## 3. Organization architecture

Map functions and conflicts:

| Function | Narrative questions |
|---|---|
| Client or political sponsor | What outcome is desired, and what cannot be admitted? |
| Broker or mission authority | Who verifies contracts, assigns teams, and withholds context? |
| Handler | Who controls information, access, payment, discipline, and extraction? |
| Intelligence | How is a target represented, and what bias or gap enters the file? |
| Logistics | Who provides identities, travel, equipment, safe housing, and medical support? |
| Field operatives | Solo, pair, cell, or team; what do they know about one another? |
| Review and sanction | What counts as failure, betrayal, collateral damage, or retirement? |
| Finance and identity | How are money, legal existence, debt, and family cover controlled? |
| Cleanup and aftermath | Who handles evidence, witnesses, public narrative, and internal blame? |

Give the organization internal incentives: budget, jurisdiction, promotion, ideological faction, personal patronage, rival teams, contractor status, and fear of exposure. A code name and a black room are not an institution.

## 4. Mission architecture

Build missions as story logic, not equipment catalogues:

1. **Stated objective**: what the operative is told to achieve.
2. **Hidden objective**: what the sponsor or handler actually needs.
3. **Access problem**: social, geographic, legal, technological, magical, or relational barrier.
4. **Preparation cost**: which cover, relationship, resource, or prior obligation must be used.
5. **Point of no return**: the moment abort becomes more dangerous than continuation.
6. **Contradiction**: new evidence makes the stated objective morally, personally, or strategically unstable.
7. **Choice**: complete, alter, expose, fake, abandon, rescue, or reverse the mission.
8. **Extraction and aftermath**: survival, payment, sanction, compromised identity, blame, and the next order.

The plan should fail through character, incomplete intelligence, institutional conflict, or established world rules. Random incompetence is not a reversal.

## 5. Professional life and cover life

Ground the AU through routine:

- Waiting, travel, sleep disruption, language, paperwork, physical recovery, money, clothing, food, laundry, neighbors, and care of a repeated object.
- Cover employment and the knowledge required to perform it under casual questioning.
- Conflicting calendars, unexplained injuries, missed family obligations, storage, housing, and communication rituals.
- The difference between a professional persona, a cover persona, and private behavior.

Let ordinary space expose operational character. Who sits facing the door, notices a changed object, overpacks, leaves no food, maintains expensive gifts, or cannot convincingly discuss an ordinary job?

## 6. Ensemble roles

Do not make a team into identical sarcastic experts. Distinguish:

- Planner who manages dependencies but may freeze when the plan loses legitimacy.
- Social operator who reads status but risks believing their own performance.
- Technical specialist who understands systems but lacks field context.
- Physical specialist whose job is access, protection, endurance, or extraction rather than generic violence.
- Local contact with independent political or personal aims.
- Handler whose loyalty is divided between operative and institution.
- Medic, document specialist, transport lead, analyst, or cleanup worker whose decisions can alter the mission.

Give each person information unavailable to the others and a reason to withhold it.

## 7. Character translation

Assign roles from behavioral anchors:

- Action-first characters may excel in rescue or field improvisation while violating mission control.
- Procedural characters may plan, analyze, document, or audit, but should face a moral trigger that defeats caution.
- Status-aware characters may handle access, cover, negotiation, or patronage; do not make elegance synonymous with competence.
- Loyal characters may become dangerous when institutions exploit their attachment.
- Proud characters may prefer a compromised mission to visible dependence.

Preserve the source relationship: former enemies may recognize each other's methods; old friends may know the tells that compromise a cover; family pressure may operate through identity and retirement rather than aristocratic costume.

## 8. Relationship engines

Assassin AUs naturally support:

- Rivals assigned the same target.
- Partners given incompatible hidden objectives.
- Spouses who discover separate employers.
- Handler and operative with unequal information and genuine attachment.
- Protected target who understands the operative's private weakness.
- Former partners divided by one abandoned extraction.
- Killer unable to complete one contract because recognition arrives too late.

Tie intimacy to professional evidence: knowledge of routines, trust during impaired visibility, willingness to accept another person's plan, a deliberately missed opportunity, shared cover behavior, or refusal to expose a weakness. Do not treat gunpoint alone as chemistry.

## 9. Set pieces

Possible large scenes include a public event with overlapping teams, embassy or gala extraction, moving-train or aircraft transfer, vertical hotel pursuit, siege inside a domestic cover, mass identity failure, mission during blackout or magical disruption, simultaneous rival operations, or a team forced to choose between objective and rescue.

Use legible zones, clocks, crowd behavior, entrances, communication limits, mistaken identities, a changing objective, and aftermath. Avoid step-by-step real-world weapon, intrusion, concealment, or evasion instruction.

## 10. Failure audit

Reject or revise when:

- The organization has no client, money, logistics, review, or internal conflict.
- Every operative is equally skilled at everything.
- Characters kill without psychological, political, bodily, or practical residue.
- A cover identity requires no labor and is never tested by ordinary people.
- The mission turns only because the briefing lied, with no deeper incentive or evidence chain.
- Dialogue consists entirely of threats, flirting, and tactical shorthand.
- "Professional" means emotionless, fashionable, and invulnerable.
- A large action scene has no spatial logic, objective change, or relational consequence.
