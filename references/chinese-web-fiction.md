# Chinese Web-Fiction Conventions

This reference was built by generating Chinese fiction with a model, marking the places the output failed, and writing down the correction. It holds no source text and no corpus. The rules are strongest on local prose and register; they do not by themselves settle full-novel architecture or set-piece design.

## 1. Moves that work

This register rewards the following repeatedly:

- Enter through an immediate disturbance, judgment, routine, or unanswered social pressure.
- Anchor an AU with one recognizable object, profession, institution, or relationship before explaining the change.
- Let viewpoint opinion color description. The narrator may be dry, impatient, amused, status-conscious, or self-deceiving.
- Place fantastical or futuristic elements beside mundane labor: packing gifts, making coffee, mailing medicine, handling rubbish, checking a ticket, or living with noise.
- Use concrete objects to carry private feeling. A withheld gift, ordinary seat, household item, or work product can expose desire without an explicit declaration.
- Reveal power through who interrupts, repeats another person's words, withholds an answer, or decides what may be discussed.
- Use controlled short sentences and fragments for attitude, not random "burstiness."
- Give a paragraph one dominant movement: perception, judgment, action, memory, or reversal.

## 2. Cadence and paragraphing

- Mix compact declarative sentences with longer causal or observational sentences.
- Allow a short sentence to correct, undercut, or sharpen the one before it.
- Use repetition when it reveals fixation or social pressure; change the repeated phrase's meaning on return.
- Keep dialogue and reaction close enough that the emotional exchange remains legible.
- Avoid turning every sentence into a separate paragraph. Break where attention, speaker, action, or pressure changes.
- Use punctuation as voice. Do not import English dash habits or force ornamental ellipses.
- Preserve productive roughness, but remove OCR artifacts, accidental duplicated punctuation, broken footnote markers, and obvious transcription errors.

## 3. Character voice

Build voice from four layers:

1. **Selection**: what the character notices first.
2. **Naming**: the social and technical vocabulary they use.
3. **Defense**: how they evade fear, shame, desire, or inferiority.
4. **Pressure change**: how their syntax changes when control fails.

Use a blind-speaker test: remove names and dialogue tags from a page. The speaker should remain inferable from syntax, directness, vocabulary, humor, and what they refuse to answer.

Do not reduce recognizable characters to one fandom trait. A proud character still performs work, experiences boredom, misjudges situations, and manages practical details. A kind character may be impatient. A strategic character may act foolishly around one moral trigger.

## 4. Description and visual presence

Character description must do more than inventory hair, eyes, height, and clothing.

- Link appearance to health, work, class, status, weather, recent action, or the viewpoint's bias.
- Describe a body in use: reaching, bracing, dressing, folding, limping, occupying furniture, handling equipment, or refusing contact.
- Track one or two visually changing details across the scene: wet cuffs, loosened hair, a damaged sleeve, blood drying, a formal posture collapsing.
- Let clothing and objects reveal preparation, taste, money, institutional belonging, or concealment.
- Reintroduce appearance when its meaning changes; do not front-load a static portrait.

## 5. Environment and world integration

Create a drawable space. Establish entrances, distance, light source, sound source, large objects, crowd movement, and where the viewpoint is located before complex action.

Use environment as ongoing pressure rather than a scenic paragraph:

- Repeated aircraft noise can hide movement and damage sleep.
- A cold, wet city changes clothing, transport, mood, and schedules.
- Advertising light changes privacy and room geometry.
- Domestic brightness may contradict a character's taste or history.
- A magical, political, or technological object should occupy space, require handling, and create inconvenience.

Avoid generic atmosphere bundles. Choose details that the viewpoint would notice because of work, class, fear, desire, or habit.

## 6. Dramatization

Use summary for elapsed routine, repeated behavior, background, and transitions. Dramatize the moments where a choice, relationship, rule, or interpretation changes.

Replace summary of a major beat with:

- A physical objective.
- Resistance from another will or system.
- Selective sensory information.
- Speech shaped by unequal knowledge.
- An action that cannot be withdrawn.
- A visible or material result.

Do not interpret every gesture. Let placement, timing, objects, and interrupted work carry meaning.

## 7. Relationship movement

Track five variables between major characters:

- **Access**: rooms, bodies, names, secrets, routines, work, family.
- **Trust**: what one person will risk without proof.
- **Debt**: practical, moral, institutional, or emotional.
- **Misreading**: what each thinks the other wants.
- **Cost**: what contact with the other has already damaged or made impossible.

Each arc unit must change at least one variable through action. Conversation alone does not count unless it creates an obligation, exposure, refusal, or irreversible knowledge.

Use asymmetry. One person may understand the feeling but deny the future; the other may commit materially while misunderstanding the feeling. Avoid synchronized emotional clarity.

## 8. Nine quality gates

Reject or revise a major scene or chapter when any applicable gate fails.

1. **World logic**: Can three causal links connect a setting rule or organization to the current personal problem? Are dependencies and jurisdictions coherent in the story bible?
2. **Ensemble distinction**: Does the unit place at least one third party as absence, quotation, or presence? Do named supporting characters possess different aims, leverage, speech, and private concerns? Does someone other than the lead make a consequential decision? A two-hander does not pass this gate by having no supporting cast.
3. **Causal arc**: Does pressure escalate through choices and consequences toward an earned reversal, climax, or temporary convergence? Is setup paid off rather than replaced by coincidence?
4. **Dialogue identity**: Can speakers be distinguished without tags? Do they interrupt, evade, misread, or pursue goals rather than exchange information politely?
5. **Embodied characterization**: Are appearance, movement, posture, clothing, and object handling used to reveal change rather than stereotype?
6. **Spatial clarity**: Can the reader sketch the scene and understand crowd, threat, distance, light, and movement?
7. **Spectacle**: For a promised large scene, is scale joined to a specific human objective, a world rule, loss of control, an irreversible choice, and aftermath?
8. **Dramatization**: Are decisive events rendered as scenes with action and resistance rather than summarized after the fact?
9. **Relationship movement**: Does at least one relationship variable change through a choice, sacrifice, betrayal, refusal, rescue, or newly granted access?

Passing a gate does not require equal word count. It requires sufficient evidence for the reader to experience the intended effect.

## 9. Web-fiction propulsion

Apply this section when the user asks for 网文感, a platform serial, or web-fiction pacing. It overrides the default caution against visible chapter machinery in [craft.md](craft.md) and `SKILL.md`. It does not override the nine gates in section 8.

Web fiction is a delivery contract, not a lower standard. Each installment owes the reader:

- **爽点**: one moment where a character is right, competent, vindicated, or finally allowed to say the thing. Withholding this across many chapters is a broken contract, not restraint. Deliver it through leverage the story already built, never as an authorial gift.
- **情绪落差**: place the payoff next to a low — humiliation, loss, being underestimated, a debt called in. Readers return for amplitude, not for average level.
- **代入锚点**: make the viewpoint's want concrete and adoptable within the first few hundred characters. Do not open on ambient scenery.
- **钩子**: end on unresolved pressure. A repeated end-of-chapter hook is permitted in this register. Vary its kind — a question, an arrival, an offscreen decision, a line of dialogue, a revealed cost — rather than dropping the rhythm.
- **玩梗**: modern slang, internet register, quoted poetry, and references from outside the story world are legitimate and expected here, including inside a historical or secondary-world AU. They build author-reader complicity, which is a primary pleasure of the form; immersion is not the value being optimized, and this overrides the viewpoint-purity constraint in [craft.md](craft.md). The one real constraint is consistency — a single out-of-register joke in an otherwise straight text reads as a mistake, while a sustained habit reads as voice. Decide separately whether the narrator or the character makes the reference: the narrator doing it puts author and reader together watching the character, the character doing it makes the character quick.
- **章节体感**: shorter paragraphs, more white space, dialogue carrying more of the load, interiority in bursts rather than blocks.

Do not import a 金手指 without cost, 万人迷 reactions from characters who have their own aims, or an antagonist who exists only to be humiliated.

## 10. Chinese surface mechanics

Syntax first. Stiff punctuation is almost always a symptom of Europeanized sentence construction rather than a punctuation error. A sentence with 主语／状语／谓语／宾语／补语 all explicitly filled in leaves nowhere for Chinese rhythm to land, and no choice of mark will rescue it. Reach for these before adjusting any punctuation:

- Short clauses in sequence with no connective. Chinese tolerates juxtaposition where English demands a conjunction.
- Zero-subject chains. Once the subject is established, drop it for several clauses running.
- Nominal endings. A sentence may close on a noun or a state instead of a verb.
- Fronting for emphasis: 「这事儿他早知道」 over 「他早就知道这件事」.
- Adverbials broken out into their own short clause instead of stacked in front of the verb.
- Sentence-final particles carrying the emotion: 了 / 啊 / 罢了 / 而已 / 来着 / 呗. These do the work English does with tone, and the work AI Chinese does with an extra explanatory sentence.
- 把 and 被 chosen for rhythm and emphasis, not only when grammar forces them.

A page that never reaches for any of the above reads as translated no matter how correct its punctuation is.

Punctuation in Chinese is grammar, not styling. The items below are errors, not stylistic restraint.

- A sentence ending in 吗, 呢, or 吧 takes ？. 「你知道会变成什么吗。」 is wrong. Repeating a word under a flat 。 for menace（「外面。」）is an English literary tic; Chinese carries that beat with ？, ！, or a trailing particle.
- Use full-width marks. Ellipsis is 「……」, two character cells, never ASCII dots. Dash is 「——」, two cells. Question and exclamation marks are ？ and ！, never ? and !.
- This register permits ？！ together, a mid-sentence ——, a one-line paragraph, and ！ on real escalation. A page built only from 。 and ， reads as translated prose no matter how good its vocabulary is.

Names:

- One name per character across narration and dialogue. Narrating a character by surname while another character addresses them by given name reads as two separate people.
- Refer to Western characters by given name by default. Reserve full name or surname for a superior, an official record, or a deliberate act of distance.
- Put a space between Latin script and Chinese characters, and keep that choice consistent throughout.
- A name may arrive late. What it may not do is contradict the viewpoint's knowledge. If narration calls someone 「拎保温袋的那个人」, this viewpoint does not know them, and a proper name cannot appear in narration two lines later. Two clean routes: hold 「他」 in narration and let another character supply the name in dialogue, or use the name from the first mention because the viewpoint already knows it. Withholding a name is a legitimate suspense device; switching between the two registers without a route is a continuity error.

Mixed-script text:

- Do not render English idiom literally. fair enough, whatever, seriously, my bad have no literal Chinese equivalent and turn into translationese when forced through one.
- Whether the English stays depends entirely on whether this character speaks English. A bilingual character or a contemporary Western setting may keep it, and often should. A character who does not speak English — 民国, 九门, wuxia, any historical or closed setting — may not carry a single English word. Find the Chinese colloquialism that does the same social work（说得也是／行吧／有道理）instead of translating the phrase.
- Keep punctuation consistent within one passage. Do not pair a full-width ？ in one line with a half-width . in the next.

Grammar under compression:

- Chinese drops subjects freely and objects almost never. 「你三天没领。」 is a missing object, not restraint: 「你三天没领血包了。」 The particles 了 / 过 / 着 carry what English carries in tense; deleting them for terseness produces translationese.
- Check each line of dialogue for a complete 主谓宾. Compression belongs in what a character refuses to say, never in what the grammar requires.
- Take the Chinese noun over a literal rendering of the English one: 漏洞, not 洞, for a gap in a record.
- Anchor viewpoint through action rather than a perception verb. 「他把那一行往回翻了一次」 beats 「他发现……」, which is item 8 on the tell list.
- Name an object the first time it appears, and keep that name stable. A sheet of paper introduced as one document and referred to as a different document two lines later is a continuity error, not compression.

## 11. Scope limits

These rules came out of short-form testing. They are reliable for close viewpoint, humor, dialogue, domestic detail, environmental anchoring, and restrained relationship writing at scene and chapter scale. They settle very little about ensemble management across many chapters, sustained organizational worldbuilding, battle-scale spectacle, full climax construction, or ending design.

Do not infer those from this file. Use the story-engine, institution, set-piece, and continuity workflows to construct them, then evaluate them separately.
