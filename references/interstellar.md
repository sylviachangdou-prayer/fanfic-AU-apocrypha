# Interstellar and Galactic-Civilization AU Guide

Use this guide for space travel, planetary survival, generation or relativistic missions, space opera, galactic empire, or distant-world political fiction. Keep it distinct from cyberpunk, whose primary pressure is networked urban power and bodily technology.

## 1. Choose the interstellar lane

Select one primary lane:

- **Near-future survival**: one planet, habitat, vessel, or rescue problem; engineering and organizational realism dominate.
- **Relativistic voyage**: time dilation, communication loss, generational separation, and mission governance dominate.
- **Deep-time encounter**: scale, cognition, alien artifacts, artificial intelligence, and epistemic uncertainty dominate.
- **Galactic civilization**: trade, transport monopoly, imperial decay, religion, ecology, dynasties, and institutions across worlds dominate.
- **Synthetic-person border case**: android labor, personhood, bounty systems, and moral classification. Use only as a secondary lane; it overlaps cyberpunk more than core interstellar fiction.

Do not combine all lanes automatically. A technically plausible Mars survival story and a feudal faster-than-light empire make different promises.

## 2. Source-work signatures

### Interstellar

Study ecological collapse, mission secrecy, family separation, gravitational time dilation, imperfect remote communication, sacrifice, and a scientific problem whose emotional meaning changes across unequal time. The core is not merely space spectacle; it is time converted into relational cost.

### The Martian

Study survival as a chain of measurable problems, repurposed equipment, logs, agricultural and habitat constraints, procedural teams on Earth, public communication, rescue politics, and failures caused by material assumptions. Competence remains interesting because each solution consumes margin and creates a new risk.

### 2001: A Space Odyssey

Study routine technology, institutional secrecy, silence and scale, human dependence on automated systems, ambiguous intelligence, procedural mismatch, and the estranging effect of precise environments. Avoid reducing the source to an "evil AI" template.

### Foundation and Asimov's connected future histories

Study imperial decline, knowledge preservation, psychohistory and its assumptions, institutional plans that outlive founders, scientific and commercial leverage, center-periphery politics, crises produced by structural conditions, hidden correction, and the threat posed by an individual or species outside the model.

### Dune

Study ecology as political economy, a transport monopoly dependent on one resource, feudal houses linked through commerce and marriage, religious engineering, colonial extraction, specialized human schools, prophecy as both belief and manipulation, and the danger of charismatic liberation narratives.

### Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?

Use as a boundary source for synthetic personhood, labor, empathy tests, bounty work, damaged ecology, artificial animals, and unstable moral classification. Do not use it to turn an interstellar story into generic neon cyberpunk.

Do not copy plots, terminology, or signature scenes. Extract mechanisms and story pleasures.

## 3. Physics and technology contract

Decide:

| Domain | Required decisions |
|---|---|
| Propulsion | Method, acceleration, fuel or energy, heat, maintenance, maneuver limits |
| Faster-than-light travel | Present or absent, access, route, failure, political control |
| Communication | Speed, bandwidth, delay, encryption, authority during silence |
| Gravity | Natural, rotational, acceleration-based, artificial, and bodily effects |
| Life support | Air, water closure, food, waste, microbes, spare capacity |
| Radiation | Exposure, shielding, monitoring, cumulative cost |
| Medicine | Surgical capacity, drugs, reproduction, quarantine, mental health |
| Automation and AI | Access, sensors, goals, override, trust, maintenance, legal status |
| Navigation | Maps, clocks, prediction, observation, uncertainty, launch windows |
| Planetary environment | Atmosphere, pressure, temperature, light, dust, gravity, biosphere |

Use numbers when scale changes a decision. Do not bury the story in calculations that never constrain action.

## 4. Mission and habitat systems

Map work rather than treating a ship as a moving room:

- Command, navigation, engineering, science, medical, communications, logistics, security, food, waste, and maintenance.
- Shift schedules, sleep, exercise, privacy, hygiene, noise, heat, vibration, and conflict over shared volume.
- Inventory ownership, substitution rules, cannibalized parts, contamination, and deferred maintenance.
- Emergency authority, mission objectives, abort criteria, succession, and who may change the plan.
- Relationship between crew, ground control, sponsors, settlers, passengers, and remote family.

Habitats need inputs, outputs, redundancy, and failure cascades. A sealed environment makes ordinary neglect political.

## 5. Galactic institutions

For galactic civilization, map at least five functions:

1. Transport and navigation.
2. Communication and records.
3. Trade, currency, credit, and insurance.
4. Law, citizenship, taxation, and dispute resolution.
5. Military protection and local enforcement.
6. Knowledge production and education.
7. Religion, ritual, and legitimacy.
8. Planetary extraction and environmental governance.

Ask which function is monopolized and what everyone else must concede to access it. A transport guild, imperial court, commercial combine, planetary house, scientific foundation, or religious order needs revenue, internal factions, succession, information limits, and local agents.

Galactic scale does not erase local government. Show how distant law becomes tariffs, docking priority, quotas, marriage, conscription, debt, language, or missing spare parts.

## 6. Planetary ecology and resource power

Define the planet as a system:

- Energy source and seasonal or orbital variation.
- Water, atmosphere, soil or substrate, food web, and waste sinks.
- Habitable zones and transport corridors.
- Indigenous life, imported life, contamination, and quarantine.
- Resource whose extraction changes ecology and political control.
- Labor required to maintain settlement.

Avoid a single-biome planet unless the mechanism justifies it. Even a desert world has elevation, temperature bands, subsurface water, storms, settlement ecologies, and engineered microclimates.

## 7. Time, communication, and family

- Track clock time for ship, planet, Earth or origin world, and message receipt.
- Let delay create independent authority, obsolete orders, rumor, and grief without confirmation.
- Record how children, partners, governments, and creditors age during travel.
- Use archived messages as objects with transmission history, censorship, compression, and changed meaning on receipt.
- A returning traveler may be biologically young but institutionally obsolete.

Do not use time dilation only for a final tearful reveal. Let it change contracts, consent, inheritance, command, and whether promises can still be honored.

## 8. AU translation

- A school rivalry may become competition between mission cohorts, habitats, planetary academies, or scientific factions.
- Noble lineage may become colony sponsorship, genetic archive access, transport shares, or founder status.
- Magic may translate into scarce navigation cognition, engineered biology, alien technology, or institutional authorization, but keep its social function and limits.
- A warrior becomes meaningful through mission role, command relation, and consequence of force in a sealed environment, not by carrying a futuristic weapon.
- Family obligation becomes sharper when travel and communication make return materially impossible.

Preserve character behavior under changed scale. Do not convert every clever character into a scientist or every proud character into an imperial heir.

## 9. Set pieces

Possible large scenes include launch failure, docking under rotation, habitat decompression, dust or radiation storm, synchronized planetary evacuation, fleet passage through a constrained route, mass communication blackout, orbital descent, generation-ship mutiny, imperial ceremony disrupted by transport collapse, first contact, or a rescue whose clocks disagree.

Build the scene with:

- A small human objective.
- Legible geometry and clocks.
- One established physical or institutional rule.
- A failure cascade rather than unrelated explosions.
- Unequal information between locations.
- An irreversible command or sacrifice.
- Survivors, debris, lost capacity, political blame, and changed relationships afterward.

## 10. Failure audit

Reject or revise when:

- The setting is generic cyberpunk moved onto a station.
- Technology appears only as names and screens, with no maintenance or constraint.
- Distance and delay disappear whenever the plot needs coordination.
- Every planet is one city or one biome.
- Galactic organizations have unlimited knowledge and no logistics.
- Engineering competence functions as magic and never produces secondary risk.
- Artificial intelligence repeats the question of whether it is human without a specific institutional or operational conflict.
- A spectacular disaster leaves no effect on inventory, authority, ecology, or relationships.
